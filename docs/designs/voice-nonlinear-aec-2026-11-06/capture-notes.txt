# Physical acoustic capture notes — 2026-11-06

The microphone became reachable in this session: the preflight captured
57,514 bytes from `MacBook Pro Microphone` and the recording had real signal
(RMS 192, peak 777 in PCM16 units). The earlier "0 bytes" blocker no longer
applies.

Two capture paths were tested:

- `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -i :1 ...` produced audio, but the timeline was
  compressed. A 4.0 s click train at 0.500 s intervals was recorded as
  0.400 s intervals, while a steady 1 kHz tone still measured 996 Hz. That is
  sample-drop distortion, not a sample-rate shift, and it destroys the
  sample-accurate alignment an adaptive canceller needs. `ffmpeg` was not used
  for the measurement.
- `sounddevice`/CoreAudio with `latency="high"` preserved the click intervals
  exactly (0.500 s, 0.500 s, 0.500 s, 0.500 s, 0.500 s, 0.495 s) and produced a
  clean 10 s capture. This is the path in
  `scripts/physical-aec-capture.py`.

Capture used:

- reference: `say -o ref.wav --data-format=LEI16@22050`, resampled to 16 kHz;
- playback: `afplay` through the built-in speakers at system output volume 90;
- microphone: built-in MacBook Pro Microphone, 48 kHz, then resampled to
  16 kHz;
- same desk, no headphones, no external microphone.

The resulting fixture is `tests/fixtures/physical-aec/` (recording, reference,
and measured alignment). The playback offset was 19,197 samples (1,199.8 ms);
the acoustic delay measured by the canceller after alignment was 2.75 ms.
